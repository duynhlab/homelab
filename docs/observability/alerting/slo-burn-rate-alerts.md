## 1. The method: multi-window, multi-burn-rate

Two failure modes need different sensitivity:

| Mode | Signature | Right detector |
|---|---|---|
| **Sudden** outage | 5xx jumps from 0 → 90% in seconds | Short window (5 min), high burn rate |
| **Slow** degradation | 5xx drifts up over hours | Long window (1–6 h), moderate burn rate |

A single threshold on a single window can't satisfy both — pick a low threshold and you flap; pick a high one and you miss the slow burn. The **multi-window multi-burn-rate** pattern from [Google SRE Workbook ch. 5](https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/) AND-gates a short window with a long window so each alert needs *both* time scales to agree before firing.

### Burn rate, in one line

```
burn_rate = (actual error fraction during window) / (1 - SLO objective)
```

`burn_rate = 1` means "you'll burn the whole 30-day budget exactly at day 30". `burn_rate = 14.4` means "you'll burn it in `30/14.4 ≈ 2 days`".

### Why exactly 14.4× and 6×?

Numbers come from the workbook and are calibrated for a **30-day** SLO window:

| Severity | Long window | Burn rate | Budget consumed if it persists for the full long window | Time-to-exhaustion if rate stays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Page** | 1 h | **14.4×** | 2% of 30-day budget in 1 h | ~2 days |
| **Ticket** | 6 h | **6×** | 5% of 30-day budget in 6 h | ~5 days |

The short window (5 min for page, 30 min for ticket) just confirms the burn is happening *right now* — both sides must be over the threshold.

For a deeper derivation see the [SRE Workbook chapter on alerting on SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/) — Sloth implements the "Alert 6 (Multiwindow, Multi-Burn-Rate Alerts)" variant.

## 2. What Sloth generates per SLO

Each `PrometheusServiceLevel` produces a `PrometheusRule` containing **recording rules**; the **2 alerts per SLO** are emitted beside it with a minimum-events guard (see [Minimum-events guard](#minimum-events-guard)). With 9 HTTP services (inventory opts out of the chart SLOs) covered by 3 SLOs each (availability, latency, and error rate), inventory by 2 gRPC SLOs, and Keycloak by 2 hand-written identity SLOs (`login-availability`, `auth-latency` — [`keycloak-login-slo.yaml`](../../../kubernetes/infra/configs/observability/sloth/keycloak-login-slo.yaml)), that is **31 SLOs / 62 alerts**.

### Minimum-events guard

A ratio over a handful of events is not a burn rate. On an idle Kind cluster,
3 slow requests out of 8 in six hours paged `CheckoutHighLatency` and failed the
K5.8 gate. Sloth v0.16's `PrometheusServiceLevel` has no field for a minimum
event count (the only alert option is `disable`). The platform therefore splits
the work:

- **Sloth** keeps the SLIs and every recording rule, so the error budget and the
  SLO dashboards are exact. `pageAlert` and `ticketAlert` are `disable: true`.
- **The alerts** are re-emitted with the names, labels (`severity`,
  `sloth_severity`, `channel`, `category`, `service`) and annotations Sloth would
  have generated. The expression is Sloth's own: page 14.4× over 5m and 1h, or 6×
  over 30m and 6h; ticket 3× over 2h and 1d, or 1× over 6h and 3d.
- **One addition:** each arm also requires at least `N` events in its long
  window, counted on the SLO's own total counter
  (`and on() sum(increase(<total>[<long window>])) >= N`). `N` defaults to 10.
  Below it, one event moves the ratio by more than 10 points.

| SLOs | Where the alerts live | `N` |
|---|---|---|
| 9 HTTP services × 2 | `slo` chart `templates/prometheusrule.yaml` (`<service>-slo-burn`) | `minEvents` (10) |
| inventory × 2, keycloak × 2 | [`sloth/slo-alerts.yaml`](../../../kubernetes/infra/configs/observability/sloth/slo-alerts.yaml) | 10 |

`sloth_id`, `sloth_slo` and `sloth_service` survive on the result series, so the
Alertmanager routes and inhibitions that match them are unchanged. Accepted
trade-off: a service that sees fewer than 10 events in an hour cannot page; the
ticket arms (1d and 3d long windows) still catch a sustained problem.

### Recording rules (per SLO, per window)

```promql
# Sliding-window SLI error ratio over 5m / 30m / 1h / 2h / 6h / 1d / 3d
slo:sli_error:ratio_rate5m{sloth_service="auth", sloth_slo="availability"}
slo:sli_error:ratio_rate1h{sloth_service="auth", sloth_slo="availability"}
slo:sli_error:ratio_rate6h{sloth_service="auth", sloth_slo="availability"}

# Period-aggregated metrics
slo:current_burn_rate:ratio{sloth_service="auth", sloth_slo="availability"}
slo:error_budget_remaining:ratio{sloth_service="auth", sloth_slo="availability"}
slo:objective:ratio{sloth_service="auth", sloth_slo="availability"}
```

### The 2 alerts (Sloth default labels)

For each SLO Sloth emits exactly two alert rules:

| Alert name pattern | Severity label | Short window | Long window | Burn rate | Wakes you up? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `<service>HighErrorRate` (page) | `severity: page` | 5 min | 1 h | 14.4× | **Yes** |
| `<service>HighErrorRate` (ticket) | `severity: ticket` | 30 min | 6 h | 6× | No — business-hours follow-up |

The alert name is reused for the SLO category (the SLI determines the meaning):

| SLO | Page-alert name (default) | What "high error rate" means here |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | `<svc>HighErrorRate` | 5xx fraction is burning the availability budget |
| Latency | `<svc>HighLatency` | Fraction of requests slower than 500 ms is burning the latency budget |

(Names are configurable through `alertNames` in the `slo` chart values; defaults above match the chart at the time of writing.)

### Standard alert labels and annotations

Every Sloth-generated alert carries:

| Field | Source | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `sloth_service` | `PrometheusServiceLevel.spec.service` | Routing & dashboards |
| `sloth_slo` | SLO name (`availability` / `latency` / `error-rate`) | Filter in Karma / Sloth UI |
| `sloth_severity` | `page` or `ticket` | Routing in VMAlertmanager |
| `severity` | mirrors `sloth_severity` | VMAlertmanager standard label |
| `team` | `sloth.extraLabels.team` (we set `platform`) | Ownership |
| `summary` / `description` | rendered with the burn rate and budget remaining | Alert payload |

## 3. Configuration — per-service overrides

SLOs are wired through the [`slo` Helm chart](https://github.com/duynhlab/helm-charts/tree/main/charts/slo), one `<service>-slo` HelmRelease per service — see [`getting_started.md`](../slo/getting_started.md) for the full flow. Alert behaviour is controlled by these values:

```yaml
labels:
  team: platform              # propagates as `team` label
  env: monitoring             # propagates as `env` label
availability:
  objective: 99.5             # changes the 14.4×/6× anchors automatically
latency:
  objective: 95.0
  threshold: "0.5"            # 500 ms — the bucket le="..."
minEvents: 10                 # guard: requests needed in each arm's long window
tiers: [...]                  # page/ticket arms; Sloth's 30-day defaults
```

**Tightening the SLO** (e.g. `99.5 → 99.9`) **automatically tightens the alerts** — the burn-rate maths is anchored on `1 - objective`, so 14.4× of a smaller error budget triggers on a smaller absolute error rate. You don't (and shouldn't) hand-edit the burn-rate thresholds.

To change *labels* on the generated alerts (route to a different Slack channel, mark as critical), edit the chart values; do not edit Sloth's output (it gets regenerated).

## 4. Where to see firing alerts

The platform exposes alerts at every layer of the pipeline. Use whichever fits the situation:

| Tool | URL | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Karma** | [karma.duynh.me](http://karma.duynh.me) | The on-call view — groups, silences, history, multi-AM aggregation. **Scaled to 0** locally: `kubectl scale deploy/karma -n monitoring --replicas=1` first |
| **VMAlert UI** | [vmalert.duynh.me](http://vmalert.duynh.me) | Inspect rule definitions, evaluation timing, last error |
| **Sloth UI** | [slo.duynh.me](http://slo.duynh.me) | SLO-centric view — which SLOs are over budget, SLI chart, burn chart. **Scaled to 0** locally: `kubectl scale deploy/sloth-ui -n monitoring --replicas=1` first |
| **Grafana** | [grafana.duynh.me](http://grafana.duynh.me) | Dashboards: "Sloth SLO Overview" (14643), "Sloth SLO Detailed" (14348) |
| **VMSingle (vmui)** | [vmui.duynh.me/vmui](http://vmui.duynh.me/vmui) | Ad-hoc PromQL on `slo:*` recording rules |

For routing semantics (group_by, receivers, silences) see [`README.md`](./README.md) (the alerting overview) — VMAlertmanager routes by severity to `slack-default`/`slack-critical` receivers; the `slack_api_url` is a placeholder pending secret injection, and PagerDuty is planned.

## 5. On-call handling

When a Sloth-generated alert fires, work the problem in this order:

### Page alert (`severity=page`) — wake-up

1. **Acknowledge in Karma** within 5 minutes. Stops re-pages.
2. **Identify scope**:
   - Open Sloth UI → service detail. Is *one* SLO burning, or all three? Is *one* service burning, or many?
   - One service, one SLO → a code/dependency issue specific to that path.
   - All services, availability SLO → infra-wide (gateway, network, DB, control plane).
3. **Correlate with deploys / config changes**:
   - Flux UI ([ui.duynh.me](http://ui.duynh.me)) — recent reconciliations.
   - Service repo Git log around the burn start time.
4. **Drill into Layer-1 alerts** for the same service in Karma — `MicroserviceHighErrorRate`, `MicroserviceHighP95Latency` etc. usually point at the proximate cause faster than the SLO alert.
5. **Mitigate**, then **silence** the SLO alert in Karma with a clear comment + expiry. Don't leave silences open-ended.

### Ticket alert (`severity=ticket`) — next business hours

1. Confirm the burn is real (Sloth UI → SLI chart). Transient bumps that didn't hit page severity can resolve on their own.
2. If the trend continues, file a ticket and treat it as planned reliability work.
3. Check [`error_budget_policy.md`](../slo/error_budget_policy.md) — if the budget is below 20%, follow the policy gates on deploys.

### Per-SLI starter checks

| SLO | First Grafana panel | First PromQL query |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | "Microservice errors" → 5xx by endpoint | `sum by(http_route)(rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_count{app="<svc>",http_response_status_code=~"5.."}[5m]))` |
| Latency | "Microservice latency" → p95 by endpoint | `histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by(le,http_route)(rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_bucket{app="<svc>"}[5m])))` |
| Error rate | "Microservice errors" → 4xx & 5xx by endpoint | same as availability with `http_response_status_code=~"4..|5.."` |

## 6. Testing & verification

### Confirm the rules exist after you enable an SLO

```bash
# PrometheusServiceLevel reconciled OK
kubectl get prometheusservicelevel -A
kubectl describe prometheusservicelevel <svc> -n monitoring   # GEN OK=true

# Sloth generated the PrometheusRule
kubectl get prometheusrule -n monitoring -l app.kubernetes.io/managed-by=sloth

# VM Operator converted it to a VMRule
kubectl get vmrule -n monitoring -l app.kubernetes.io/managed-by=sloth
```

### Confirm VMAlert picked it up

Open [vmalert.duynh.me](http://vmalert.duynh.me) and search for the alert name (`<svc>HighErrorRate` etc.) — you should see the rule, the last evaluation timestamp, and the current value.

### Force the alert to fire (synthetic load)

Real traffic is the safest source. If you need to synthesise traffic for testing burn-rate alerts, generate it ad-hoc against the public endpoints via the edge — **do not** invent endpoints (`/api/error`, `/api/slow`) — none exist in the services. To exercise the availability SLO, target a real handler and induce errors via fault injection or by toggling a feature flag.

### Test the routing without real traffic

```bash
# Send a fake alert directly to VMAlertmanager
kubectl -n monitoring port-forward svc/vmalertmanager-victoria-metrics 9093 &

curl -X POST http://localhost:9093/api/v2/alerts -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '[{
  "labels": { "alertname": "AuthHighErrorRate", "severity": "page", "sloth_service": "auth", "sloth_slo": "availability" },
  "annotations": { "summary": "synthetic test" },
  "startsAt": "2026-01-01T00:00:00Z",
  "endsAt":   "2026-01-01T00:05:00Z"
}]'

# Verify in Karma
open http://karma.duynh.me
```

## 7. Known limitations / gotchas

- **Burn-rate maths assumes a stable traffic volume.** At very low RPS one bad request can blow the short-window burn rate. The [minimum-events guard](#minimum-events-guard) keeps an arm silent until its long window holds at least 10 events; above that, low traffic can still be noisy.
- **Mixed-traffic services** (background jobs piggybacking on the same `http_server_request_duration_seconds`) pollute the SLI. Filter at the SLI level (`http_route!~"…"`) if needed.
- **The SLI is a 30-day rolling window.** A burn from 25 days ago still counts toward today's budget. Don't be surprised if the budget is low even when current traffic is healthy.
- **Notification delivery is pending** — VMAlertmanager has `slack-default`/`slack-critical` receivers wired, but `slack_api_url` is a placeholder. Until it is set (via External Secrets / OpenBAO), "page" severity = "appears in Karma faster". Track via the [Future Roadmap in `alerting/README.md`](./README.md#future-roadmap).
- **Dashboards in VMAlert UI** show rule definitions only; "for live SLI shape, prefer the Sloth UI or Grafana SLO dashboards."
